## Project Overview

This project examines how reported human trafficking cases progressed through the justice system between **2003 and 2023**.

The Justice System Response Dashboard focuses on the relationship between **prosecutions and convictions**, allowing the analysis to move beyond the number of trafficking cases reported and examine how those cases translated into justice outcomes.

The dashboard provides a visual view of prosecution and conviction trends across countries, regions, and years.

---

## Problem Statement

Reporting a trafficking offence is only the beginning of the justice process.

A case may be investigated and prosecuted without necessarily resulting in a conviction. Understanding this gap is important when evaluating how trafficking cases move through the justice system.

The analysis therefore focused on questions such as:

- How have prosecutions and convictions changed over time?
- Which countries recorded the highest number of convictions?
- Which regions recorded the highest prosecution and conviction figures?
- What does the gap between prosecutions and convictions reveal?
- How does the conviction rate vary across countries?

---

## Key Objectives

The analysis aimed to:

- Examine prosecution and conviction trends between 2003 and 2023.
- Compare prosecutions with recorded convictions.
- Identify countries and regions with higher justice-system figures.
- Analyse conviction rates across countries.
- Visualize the progression from prosecution to conviction.
- Identify areas that may require further investigation.

---

## Data Summary

The dataset covers reported human trafficking justice-system activity between **2003 and 2023**.

The dashboard presents:

- **284,791 total prosecutions**
- An overall **39% conviction rate**
- Country-level prosecution and conviction figures
- Regional comparisons
- Yearly prosecution and conviction trends
- Conviction-rate comparisons

The dashboard identifies **India** as the country with the highest recorded convictions and **Asia** as the leading region by conviction figures.

## Dashboard Overview

### 1. Prosecution and Conviction Trends

The time-series visualization compares prosecution and conviction figures across the years.

This makes it possible to identify periods where prosecution and conviction numbers increased, decreased, or moved differently.

The dashboard identifies **2005 as the year with the highest recorded conviction figure**.

---

### 2. Prosecutions by Region

The regional comparison shows significant differences in reported prosecution figures.

**Asia recorded the highest prosecution figure**, followed by other regions represented in the dataset.

This provides a broader geographical perspective on where trafficking cases are entering the justice system.

---

### 3. Convictions by Region

The regional conviction analysis shows how recorded convictions are distributed geographically.

Asia also records the highest conviction figures in the dashboard.

However, high conviction numbers should be interpreted alongside prosecution volumes rather than viewed in isolation.

---

### 4. Conviction Rate

The dashboard provides a country-level view of conviction rates.

The overall conviction rate stands at approximately **39%**.

This metric provides an additional perspective beyond simply counting prosecutions and convictions because it considers the relationship between the two.

---

### 5. Prosecution-to-Conviction Gap

One of the most important analytical perspectives in this dashboard is the difference between prosecutions and convictions.

A high number of prosecutions does not automatically translate into the same number of convictions.

This gap can provide a starting point for further investigation into factors such as evidence, case preparation, judicial processes, and reporting practices.

---

## Key Findings

### 1. Prosecutions exceed convictions

The dashboard shows a substantial difference between the number of recorded prosecutions and convictions.

The overall conviction rate of **39%** indicates that a significant proportion of prosecuted cases did not result in a recorded conviction within the dataset.

### 2. Asia recorded the highest justice-system figures

Asia recorded the highest regional prosecution and conviction figures shown in the dashboard.

### 3. India recorded the highest country-level convictions

The country comparison identifies **India** as the leading country by recorded convictions.

### 4. Justice outcomes fluctuate over time

The yearly analysis shows that prosecution and conviction figures vary considerably across the period rather than following a consistent pattern.

### 5. Conviction rate provides additional context

Looking only at the number of convictions can be misleading.

The conviction-rate view provides another way of comparing justice outcomes by considering convictions relative to prosecutions.

## Recommendations

### Strengthen Case Preparation

Improving investigation and evidence-gathering processes may help strengthen trafficking cases as they move through prosecution.

### Improve Justice-System Capacity

Specialised training for law-enforcement personnel, prosecutors, and other relevant justice-sector professionals can support stronger case handling.

### Investigate the Prosecution-to-Conviction Gap

Countries and regions with significant differences between prosecutions and convictions could benefit from deeper investigation into the causes.

### Improve Data Consistency

More consistent reporting across countries would make international comparisons more reliable.

### Monitor Justice Outcomes

Tracking both prosecution and conviction figures over time can provide a clearer picture of how effectively trafficking cases progress through the justice system.

---

## Limitations

The figures represent **recorded activity within the dataset** and should not be interpreted as a complete measurement of every trafficking case or justice outcome globally.

Differences in legal systems, definitions, reporting practices, investigation procedures, and data availability can influence comparisons between countries.

A lower conviction figure does not necessarily mean that a country's justice system is less effective without understanding the wider context behind the data.

---

## Conclusion

The Justice System Response Dashboard demonstrates how data can be used to examine trafficking beyond the initial offence.

By comparing **prosecutions, convictions, conviction rates, countries, regions, and yearly trends**, the analysis provides a clearer view of how reported trafficking cases progress through the justice system.

The most important takeaway is the **gap between prosecution and conviction**.

Understanding that gap can help identify where deeper investigation, stronger case preparation, improved reporting, or additional justice-system support may be required.
